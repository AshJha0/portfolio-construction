# Cost-aware allocation on synthetic data

**The data are synthetic.** The 1250 daily returns in `data/asset_returns.csv` come from `data/generate_data.py` (a three-factor model with Student-t shocks, seed 42). No result on this page is evidence of an edge in any real market. What the page does show is how the construction rules in `portopt.research.strategies` behave once trading costs, weight drift and sampling error are accounted for, using instruments that would apply unchanged to real data.

Generated by `python python/studies/cost_aware_allocation.py`; the full grid is in `cost_aware_allocation.csv`. Rerunning the script reproduces both files byte for byte.

## Method

We use `portopt.research.run_backtest_net` with the pinned schedule: an expanding estimation window starting at 500 days and a rebalance every 63 days, which gives 12 rebalances and a 750-day holdout. Between rebalances the portfolio is buy and hold, so weights drift and the next rebalance pays for undoing that drift. Each rebalance, including the initial build from cash, is charged `cost_bps` on the traded risky notional `sum_i |w_new_i - w_drift_i|`. A no-trade band skips a rebalance when that L1 distance is below `band`. Cash earns rf = 0.02 a year (rf/252 a day). We ran 12 rules at cost_bps in {0, 5, 10, 25} and band in {0, 0.05, 0.1}, 144 configurations in all.

Sharpe ratios are annualized `(252 mean(r) - rf) / (sqrt(252) std(r))` on net daily returns. Standard errors are Lo (2002) under iid normal returns and Mertens (2002) under iid non-normal returns, both scaled by sqrt(252); neither allows for serial correlation. PSR is the Bailey and Lopez de Prado (2012) probability that the true Sharpe ratio exceeds zero. DSR (Bailey and Lopez de Prado 2014) raises that hurdle to the expected maximum Sharpe ratio of N = 144 trials, using the cross-sectional variance of the 144 daily Sharpe estimates (annualized standard deviation 0.30, hurdle SR0 = 0.81 annualized). Counting every configuration as a trial overstates the number of independent trials, since the cost and band variants of one rule are highly correlated, so the DSR here is conservative. The paired test against equal weight is the Ledoit and Wolf (2008) delta-method test with a Newey-West Bartlett kernel; the bootstrap interval is a stationary bootstrap (mean block 10 days, 2000 resamples, seed 42), percentile 95%.

Rules: `max_sharpe_lo` is the best Sharpe point of a 25-point long-only frontier; `tangency` is the unconstrained tangency portfolio, falling back to the unconstrained minimum-variance portfolio when it is undefined; `tangency_cap2` moves it toward minimum variance until gross leverage is at most 2; the `_lw` suffix means Ledoit-Wolf constant-correlation shrinkage of the covariance; `black_litterman` uses `data/views.json` and the market caps.

## Conclusion

1. Costs bite in proportion to turnover. The unconstrained tangency rule trades 12.3 times NAV per rebalance. At 8 of 12 rebalances the risk-free rate is above the history's minimum-variance return, tangency is undefined and the rule holds minimum variance; at the others the tangency portfolio exists but rf sits close to that return and gross leverage reaches 94 times NAV. Its net Sharpe falls from 0.92 at 0 bp to 0.69 at 25 bp, a cost of 11.8% of NAV a year, and its maximum drawdown is 43%. Capping gross leverage at 2 cuts turnover to 0.32 per rebalance. The long-only rules lose at most 0.06 of Sharpe between 0 and 25 bp.
2. A gross backtest flatters high-turnover rules. At 0 bp the tangency rule leads the best long-only rule (`min_var_lo`) by 0.22 of Sharpe; at 25 bp the lead is 0.03, with roughly 7 times the drawdown.
3. For the 7 rules that trade less than 0.1 of NAV per rebalance, a band of 0.1 at 25 bp cuts annual turnover to between 0% and 60% of the band-0 level, and moves their net Sharpe by at most 0.04, in either direction. For the tangency rule the band barely changes turnover (46.8 against 46.8 a year) because its trades come from changes in the target, not from drift.
4. Without a band, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage changes no net Sharpe ratio by more than 0.003; with a band the gap reaches 0.05, because a small change in the target can decide whether a rebalance clears the band. With at least 500 observations on 8 assets the sample covariance is already well conditioned, so shrinkage has little to correct.
5. None of the differences is statistically meaningful. With a 750-day holdout the standard error of an annualized Sharpe ratio is about 0.58, as large as the spread between the rules. The largest PSR(0) is 0.95, the largest DSR across all 144 configurations is 0.58, and the smallest p-value of a paired test against equal weight is 0.15 before any correction for the 132 comparisons made.
6. What this does not show: anything about real markets, any rule's out-of-sample edge, or the right cost level for a given desk. The cost model is linear in traded notional with no market impact, borrowing is at rf, and the tangency fallback is a choice of ours that drives its turnover. What it does show is that the instruments (drifted-weight accounting, explicit costs, Sharpe standard errors, PSR, DSR and paired tests) are in place, and that on a 750-day sample they do not support calling any of these rules a winner.
